Parent FAQs

Screen Time, Media Literacy & Parental Control Apps – Made Simple

How much screen time / media use is actually normal for kids? +

There’s no one-size-fits-all magic number for when screen time crosses into "unhealthy" territory. It really depends on your child's age, and *what* they’re doing online matters just as much as how long they’re doing it. While you can set time limits on iPhones, iPads, Samsung, or Android devices, let’s be real: Clever kids almost always find ways around them. That’s why it’s best to bring your child into the conversation: sit down together and chat about which apps and content make sense and which ones aren't so great. Learn more in our age-appropriate screen time guides and handbooks for parents.

What is media literacy, and why does my child need it? +

Media literacy is all about helping kids navigate apps, the internet, and AI content safely, critically, and confidently—teaching them to spot red flags, double-check sources, and make smart choices on their own. It also involves self-regulation: learning to manage those strong "I want it *now*" impulses and compulsive habits. Media savvy doesn't happen overnight; it’s a skill built step by step. The earlier parents dive in and get even a basic handle on digital parenting, the better. Learn more in our media literacy guides for parents.

Why aren't parental control or screen time apps like Helmit or Family Link enough to keep my child safe online? +

Parental control apps (also called monitoring or tracking apps) are easy for tech-savvy kids to bypass, have blind spots, and don't actually teach kids how to stay safe on their own. Plus, once your kids hit their teen years, they're going to push back hard against having tracker apps on their phones. That’s why Cybear uses playful, interactive training to help kids spot online risks for themselves—no helicopter parenting required. It’s hands-down the best way to proactively protect them.

Learning to recognise these patterns — in an age-appropriate, practical way — is essential. It helps kids understand the intentions and risks behind what they're seeing. That's exactly what Cybear helps them do.

Reading situations and people better, knowing how to respond — protecting yourself and others online

When kids understand how to behave in different situations and can better read the motivations of others, self-protection comes naturally. Whether it's bullying in the class WhatsApp group, a sketchy account in a game chat, or hostile messages from peers.

For every one of these situations, there's a best way to respond.

Kids who learn to think critically and push back against harmful situations are better equipped to protect themselves — and look out for others too.

Spotting risks and danger signs early — so nothing gets in the way of staying safe online

Kids and teens act fast online — sometimes without thinking. Sharing personal details or family information, using weak passwords, downloading infected files, or saying something in a chat they'll later regret.

Learning to read a situation — and spot someone else's intentions — before things go wrong is a skill kids can build through practice.

Age-appropriate behaviour exercises don't just make kids more careful — they teach them to genuinely look after themselves. Parents learn something along the way too.
