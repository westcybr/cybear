Summer Reset

Kids Summer Digital Detox Guide

A Parent's Browsable Handbook for Screen-Free Adventures

Let's be honest: summer holidays are the absolute best time to break those stubborn screen habits. Without school schedules or homework routines, we have long, unstructured days stretching out before us. Perfect for a reset—no battle of wits required.

Why Summer Is the Perfect Reset Time

The guiding idea here isn't to launch strict ban on screens. Instead, it’s about strategically swapping segments of the day with real-world activities that offer the exact same psychological rewards.

Apps and video games are masterfully designed to satisfy core human needs: novelty, challenge, autonomy, social connection, achievement, surprise, and intense sensory stimulation. If we want kids to put down the controller, our real-world alternatives need to trigger those same feelings.

How to use this guide:

Check our quick Dos & Don'ts, lock down a simple Family Media Plan, then dive into our activity catalog.

The Ultimate Parent Goal: Reaching a "flow state." This is that cognitive state where kids get so deeply absorbed in what they are doing that apps, notifications, and games completely vanish from their minds.

Activity Ideas

🌲 Adventure & Exploration
  • Backyard safari: Find and log 15 different insects or plant species. Craft a small documentation booklet from these findings.
  • Neighborhood treasure hunt: Written clues leading to a small prize.
  • Geocaching outing: Use a free geocaching app for kids to find real hidden caches nearby (it's alright that you are using screens while exploring) or explore points of interest.
  • Build a secret base: Build a hidden base in the woods or at the back of the garden. Let them draw some sketches, visit a DIY store
  • "Somewhere new" day: Pick a new park, trail, or neighborhood none of you have visited and go explore it.
  • Waterside expedition: Creek, river, or lakeside expedition. Collect rocks, twigs or leaves, skip stones, and craft an artwork or decoration from these items.
  • Compass hike: Chose a rather offbeat hiking area with lots of untouched nature, a few trails and nice sceneries. Print top views of google maps around certain trail junctions (don't mark them). Teach your kids how to use compass and map to find their way to the destination point.
  • Flashlight night walk: Forest night walk with flashlights, looking for nocturnal animals or listening for owls.
  • Cloud-spotting challenge: Lie in the grass, make photos and name shapes. Print these photos, and you can draw something funny or create cutouts.
  • Hand-drawn park map: Make a hand-drawn map of your local park/forest, marking landmarks and "secret spots."
🔨 Build & Create
  • Giant cardboard fortress: Build a giant cardboard fort (Ikea or supermarket cardboard), decorate it with wax markers, charcoal or paint.
  • Cardboard marble run: Design and race a marble run using cardboard tubes and tape.
  • DIY bird feeder: Build and hang a simple bird feeder (pinecone + peanut butter + birdseed, or a milk-carton feeder).
  • Homemade kite: Fly a homemade kite (there are 15-minute paper-and-stick kite tutorials online).
  • DIY Perpetuum Mobile: Create a crazy machine that keeps moving. A lot of ideas on YouTube. All household utensils, tools and craft materials work. Get an OSB board or even a light LDF chipboard sheet, hammer, nails, screws, tape, string. Ready, go!
  • Character rock painting: Collect large pebbles and rocks, paint these as funky game, cartoon or movie characters to place them on the balcony or in the garden later (make photos to share online or for the family photo album).
  • LEGO showdown: Set up a LEGO challenge (build a tall building, vehicle, scene or game character).
  • Dinosaur mini world: Build a fairy garden or a mini dinosaur world in a plant pot or garden corner.
  • Painted rock characters: Paint rocks with faces or patterns and hide them around the neighborhood for others to find.
  • Popsicle stick catapult: Build a small catapult from popsicle sticks and rubber bands, test-fire cotton balls.
  • Backyard water run: Design a DIY water obstacle course in the garden with buckets, hoses, and tarps.
  • Invent a board game: Invent a brand-new board game together, including hand-drawn cards and rules.
  • Weekend carpentry project: Build a bee hotel, small garden bench, or plant shelf as a weekend project. All paint colors are allowed!
  • Create your own paper: With just a blender, sieve/mesh and some old paper scraps craft your own paper. (search for tutorials online)
  • Good ol soap box car: Take a few weeks: plan and build your dream soap box car.
  • DIY Minigolf parkour: Build your own mini-golf parcour from old bottles, boxes, cardboards, ramps and whatever slumbers in the basement or attic.
💧 Water Fun (Great for Hot Days)
  • Sprinkler Olympics: Garden sprinkler "Olympics" — relay races running through the sprinkler.
  • Homemade slip-and-slide: Homemade slip-and-slide with a tarp, hose, and dish soap.
  • Water balloon baseball: Water balloon baseball (hit balloons with a bat instead of a ball).
  • Sponge relay: Sponge relay race: carry water in a sponge from bucket to bucket.
  • Stopwatch splash run: Bucket-carrying obstacle course, timed with a stopwatch.
  • Car & bike wash fight: Wash the family bikes or the car together as a "job that's actually a water fight."
  • Bottle cap boat races: Floating boat race with homemade boats (bottle caps, sticks, foil).
  • Ice treasure excavation: "Ice treasure" excavation: freeze small toys in a block of ice, let kids chip them out.
  • Giant DIY bubbles: Giant soap bubbles using a DIY large-wand bubble mix.
🎨 Creative & Artistic Challenges
  • Chalk city driveway: Sidewalk chalk "city": draw an entire town on the driveway, streets included.
  • Spin painting: Drip paint on a rotating canvas (disc shaped). Parents can use a cake turntable or build a rotating disc with a small battery-powered 6V motor
  • Funny Face Masks: Craft DIY masks from plain white/colored paper or printouts. Don't forget that creatively painted, glued, and hand drawn masks are the most fun.
  • DIY wind chimes: Collect old tin cans, shells, twigs, keys or spoons. Paint to your liking and craft a wind chime for your balcony or terrace.
  • Superhero Cardboard Standee: Invent a superhero: Sketch it out first. Use acrylic, wax or water color paint for super hero clothing, draw details, cut out the silhouette, add stands.
  • Family comic strip: Create a family comic strip, one panel drawn by each person.
  • 5-Scene movie studio: Make a short stop-motion or live-action movie on a phone (with a written 5-scene script first).
  • LEGO/Playmobil stop-motion movie: Build up an enticing scene in LEGO or Playmobil and direct a stop-motion short story (shooting with smartphone is allowed)
  • Photo hunt: Photography scavenger hunt: name a concept or list of 10 things to photograph (something... red, tiny, smelly, yummy, odd, etc.). Print these photos and create a collage or photobook.
  • Wall art: If you decide to repaint a room or wall, let your kids to their magic first. Drawing and painting on walls is always exciting. You can paint over it a few weeks later if your kids allow it.
  • DIY artwork frame: Ideate a concept and let the kids draw/paint on a thick sheet of paper or canvas with the aim to hang it onto a wall at your home.
🏃 Movement & Energy
  • Backyard Olympics: Backyard Olympics: 5 events (long jump, sack race, egg-and-spoon, sprint, three-legged race), medals optional.
  • Ninja warrior obstacle course: Ninja Warrior–style obstacle course using furniture, cushions, and garden furniture.
  • Bike tour: Family bike ride to a destination (ice cream shop, park, viewpoint).
  • Frisbee golf course: Frisbee golf using trees or garden features as "holes." Can also place cardboard boxes for that.
  • Playground parkour: Simple parkour challenge in the garden or a local playground (jump, vault, balance). Make a slow motion video of it.
  • Kayak tour: Get your own or rent a kayak and do a Kayak tour with some picnic stopovers.
  • Family dance-off: Family dance battle with a shared music playlist.
  • Mini-golf or bowling: Book a minigolf parkour or bowling lane. Does not require too much skill, and can easily combine with food and yummy snacks.
  • Mini-sports bracket: Mini sports tournament (badminton, table tennis, or football) with a bracket.

🎭 Imagination & Role-Play
  • Pirate voyage: Pirate adventure: map, "treasure," costumes from household items.
  • Secret agent briefing: Secret agent mission with assigned tasks and a "briefcase" of gadgets (a torch, notepad, walkie-talkies).
  • Jungle explorer day: Jungle explorer day: crawl through "vines" (blankets/ropes), track "animal footprints."
  • Time-travel afternoon: Time-travel day: pick an era and dress/act accordingly for an afternoon.
  • Backyard campout: Camp overnight in the garden (tent, sleeping bags, flashlights).
  • Dragon hunt: Dragon hunt with clues leading to a hidden "egg" or "hoard."
  • Detective mystery house: Detective mystery: hide clues around the house leading to a solved "case."
  • UFO crash investigation: Alien-landing scenario with a "crashed ship" (cardboard box) to investigate.
  • Gourmet restaurant night: Restaurant role-play: kids write a menu, "cook," and serve the family.
🍕 Food Adventures
  • DIY pizza night: Homemade pizza night. Kids choose and add their own toppings, unconventional solutions welcome.
  • Ice cream bag laboratory: "Ice cream laboratory": make simple no-churn ice cream in a bag, test flavor combinations.
  • Blind eating challenge: Blind taste of various snacks, fruits and little bites. Guess the food blindfolded. Make a score chart.
  • Build-your-own taco bar: Build-your-own taco bar with bowls of toppings laid out.
  • Campfire foil-pack meals: Campfire or grill cooking with lots of meat, veggies and skewers. Also bring long sticks for marshmallows and campfire bread.
  • Nature picnic: Picnic in a new location (a park you've never been to, a rooftop, a forest clearing).
  • "Funny face" sandwiches: Make "funny face" sandwiches using vegetables and cheese as features.
  • Neighbourly cookie delivery: Bake cookies together and deliver a batch to a neighbour.
  • Candy ice pops: Craft ice-cream, use food coloring and little fruit pieces or candy that get enclosed inside the ice pops.
🌱 Nature & Garden Projects
  • Kids' veggie patch: Plant a vegetable or herb patch together and track its growth. Kids have to take care of the plants every day.
  • Nature journal pressing: Collect and press interesting leaves into a homemade nature journal.
  • Stacked bee hotel: Build a bug hotel from stacked twigs, pinecones, and hollow stems.
  • Birdwatching: Get out into nature with some binoculars or good camera and start identifying birds.
  • Make jam from harvested fruits: Harvest cherries, pears, apples, berries or Mirabelle plums and try making a fruit jam (peeling, deseeding, cooking, sweetening)
  • Kid's Pizza garden: Kids grow their own pizza ingredients. Plant tomatoes, basil, oregano and other herbs or veggies commonly found on pizzas.
  • Playful garden stepping stones: Let your kids collect, paint and decorate some garden stepping stones.
  • Worm Composting Bin: Find a large sturdy plastic box that you setup (can also paint the outside) for worm composting.
🧹 Family Missions & Household Projects
  • Beat-the-timer tidy up: 30-minute "beat the timer" house tidy-up challenge, done as a team.
  • Epic driveway car wash: Family car wash, treated as an event rather than a chore.
  • Garden bed makeover: Garden makeover: weed, replant, or rearrange one bed or corner together.
  • Cozy reading corner design: Redesign one room or a reading corner together.
  • Neighborhood toy swap: Organize a toy swap with neighborhood families.
  • Second hand sale: Go through unused items and decide together whats to let go. Kids can decorate the background for the photos used to sell the items online.
  • Storage clean-up tournament: Garage or storage-room clean-up race, sorted into keep/donate/toss.
  • Upcycled terracotta pots: Paint or upcycle old flower pots.
  • Time capsule: Make a family time capsule to open in one or five years.
  • Attic treasure hunt: Clear out a cupboard or the cellar/attic and turn it into a "treasure hunt" (whoever finds item X gets a small reward).
  • Photo book: Go through unsorted photos in storage, select and glue good ones into a photo book (10 to 15 every day).
  • Travel Map: Create a playful world map that contains different notes, stickers, souvenir gimmicks, pins. You can connect the pins with string and stick little journey time labels or clamp photos onto them.
📚 Quiet & Calm Alternatives
  • Chapter book serialization: Read a physical book together, or aloud as a family (pick a chapter book to serialize over the summer).
  • Jigsaw puzzle table: Do a puzzle marathon. Set up a jigsaw puzzle on a table that stays out all week.
  • Illustrated daily journal: Illustrated journal or diary: draw or write about the best moment of the day.
  • Board game tournament: Card and board game rotation (a different game each evening).
  • Audiobook listening circles: Listen to music or an audio story together with no screen involved.
  • Magic trick rehearsal: Learn and perform a magic trick (many simple ones need just cards or coins).
  • Summer goals manifesto: Write and decorate a "manifesto" of summer goals or house rules together.
  • Learn and play chess: A great game for those calm times. Takes a while to master, but why not start small with a few rules and moves.

  • Library reading programs: Join a local library reading program or book club for kids.
  • Active physical clubs: Sign up for a class or club with a physical focus: tennis, swimming, climbing, a running club.
  • Zoo & Aquarium: Visit your local zoo or aquarium. Maybe even collect some ideas for home decoration.
  • Kid's Museum: Pick a Technology & Science museum, a kids museum or a diverse contemporary museum to visit (some museums have kids workshops)
  • Farm Workshop/Day Visit a nearby farm that organsises workshops/activities for kids, incl. crafting or planting workshops
  • Community sports match: Organize a neighborhood football, volleyball or frisbee match.
  • Multi-family picnics: Picnic together with another family at a cool playground that you rarely visit (too far away).
  • Backyard cinema night: Outdoor movie night in the garden with a projector and a sheet.
  • Campfire storytelling rounds: Campfire storytelling evening, each person adds to a shared story.
  • Family talent expo: Family talent show, prepared over a few days.
  • Local volunteering: Community volunteering. A park or beach cleanup, food bank, or animal shelter visit.
  • Grandparent workshop: Visit grandparents or extended family for an activity-based afternoon (baking, gardening, games).

Running activities throughout the whole summer

Jar Lottery

Fill a jar with 50–100 folded slips of paper, each naming one activity from the above activities. Draw one or two each day.

Social Media Fast

Pick one or two fixed days a week, or a full weekend once a month, with no scrolling or social apps at all. Announce it in advance as part of the Family Media Plan so it does not come off as a surprise.

Digital Hibernation

For teens especially, a short (3–5 day) full break from social media (framed as "recharging," not punishment) can reset compulsive checking habits. Best scheduled around a trip, camp, or a heavily activity-filled stretch so the days are already full.

Important: Find screen replacing activities that match their preferences

Replace screen habits with real-world versions that recreate the same creativity, challenge, rewards, and connection kids enjoy in their favourite apps.

Putting It Into Practice

Let kids pick: Present 3 to 4 options from a category rather than assigning one — choice is what makes it stick.

Match energy and age: High-movement categories suit younger, energetic kids; quieter categories suit downtime or reluctant teens.

Keep a rainy-day shortlist: Pre-select a handful of indoor options so a change in weather doesn't send everyone back to a screen by default.

Rotate, don't repeat: Cycling through categories across the week keeps novelty high, which is exactly the reward screens were providing.

Celebrate small wins: A simple "detox streak" chart or verbal acknowledgment ("three screen-free afternoons this week!") reinforces the habit without needing an elaborate reward system.

What to Do — and What to Avoid

✓ The Dos:

  • Match their digital interests: If they love gaming because it’s fast and challenging, don't replace them with "go read a book." Pivot to high-octane scavenger hunts, obstacle courses, or building a backyard robot instead.
  • Offer options, not orders: Give them a small menu of choices. Kids are infinitely more likely to dive in if they feel like they made the final call.
  • Start small: You don't need a cold-turkey, screen-free summer. Start with one free afternoon, a device-free evening, or a couple of dedicated days a week. Small wins build lasting momentum.
  • Team up with other parents: It is ten times easier to unplug when their best friends are doing it too. Put a group of screen-free kids together, and boredom solves itself instantly.

✕ The Don'ts:

  • Don't issue absolute demands: Avoid making activities a non-negotiable must-do. Keep a few specific ideas ready and involve them in the choice.
  • Don't frame it as a punishment: This isn't a penalty box. The goal is not to take screens away; it’s all about doing something fun together. Keep the energy positive.
  • Don't skip the prep work: Sit down and agree on clear boundaries (see the Media Plan below) ahead of time, when everyone is calm. It saves you from exhausting daily negotiations.
  • Don't expect instant cheers: Expect some complaints and sighs during the first few days. That’s totally normal! Kids need some time to recalibrate and rediscover how to adapt.

The Family Media Plan (Set This Up First)

Before you jump into the fun stuff, sit down together and establish your family boundaries. Agreeing on these rules collectively makes them much easier to protect.

🚫 Tech-Free Zones

Keep devices strictly away from the dinner table, bedrooms, and short car rides.

⏰ Tech-Free Times

Set boundaries, such as the first hour after waking up, after 7:00 PM, or during meals.

📅 Detox Days

Pick one fixed day a week for a "Social Media Fast." Exceptions can always be made for calling grandparents!
